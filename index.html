SECURITY EDUCATION

Wi-Fi Deauthentication Storms (DoS)

A **Deauthentication Attack** is a form of Denial-of-Service (DoS) that targets communication between a client device (laptop, phone) and a wireless Access Point (AP).

Under the standard **802.11 Wi-Fi protocol**, management frames (such as connection requests, disassociations, and deauthentications) were designed to be broadcast in plaintext without encryption or signatures. An attacker can craft a spoofed deauthentication frame with a transmitter address mimicking your home router, and send it to the client device. Since the client thinks the packet is genuine, it instantly breaks the Wi-Fi connection.

Standard Loophole: The 802.11w amendment introduced PMF (Protected Management Frames), which encrypts these management packets. However, many older IoT devices, legacy printers, and routers do not support PMF, leaving them vulnerable to simple deauth floods!

How our ESP32 Sniffer Detects It:

The ESP32 is set into **promiscuous mode**, meaning its radio antenna listens to all raw 802.11 packets floating in the air on a specific channel, rather than parsing only those directed to its own MAC address. The firmware searches for frames where the Type is `0x00` (Management) and Subtype is `0x0C` (Deauthentication). If the rate of these specific packets surges past our `DEAUTH_ALERT_THRESHOLD` (5/sec), a localized denial-of-service attack is confirmed and flagged.

Evil Twin Access Point Cloning

An **Evil Twin Attack** is a rogue wireless Access Point (AP) that has been configured to broadcast the exact same network name (SSID) as a legitimate, trusted network in the vicinity.

Wireless devices automatically store whitelists of previously joined Wi-Fi networks and attempt to connect to the strongest signal matching a saved SSID. An attacker can set up a high-power rogue access point right outside a building with a cloned SSID. Once your phone or laptop connects, the attacker can sniff all your unencrypted HTTP web traffic, run redirect phishing campaigns, or harvest credentials.

How our ESP32 Scanner Detects It:

At regular intervals, the ESP32 pauses packet sniffing and performs an **Active Network Scan** across the entire 2.4GHz spectrum. The firmware compiles a database of all broadcasting access points and tests them against custom heuristic security audits:

  • MAC Address Audits: If a scanned access point's SSID matches our whitelisted home SSID but its MAC Address (BSSID) does not match the true router's MAC, it flags an Evil Twin.
  • Security Downgrades: If your home Wi-Fi is configured as WPA2-PSK (Encrypted), but a scanned AP with the exact same SSID is Open (no password) or using weak WEP encryption, it is instantly identified as a rogue access point.
